# Marketing Psychology — Behavioral Triggers in Copy

Applying established behavioral principles deliberately and honestly — not manipulation, and not principles applied where they don't fit the actual offer.

## Core principles and correct application

- **Social proof** — works best with specific, verifiable numbers ("2,400 businesses use this") over vague claims ("thousands of happy customers"). Specificity reads as more credible and is harder to fake, which matters for trust.
- **Scarcity/urgency** — only apply where genuinely true (actual limited inventory, actual deadline). Fake scarcity ("only 2 left!" that resets on refresh) destroys trust once noticed and is illegal in some jurisdictions as a deceptive practice — never fabricate this.
- **Loss aversion** — framing a benefit as "what you'd lose by not acting" is often more persuasive than the equivalent gain-framed statement, but overuse across every touchpoint reads as manipulative pressure rather than persuasive.
- **Anchoring** — showing a higher reference price before the actual price (e.g. crossed-out original price) shifts perceived value; must be a real prior price, not a fabricated one, for both ethical and (in many markets) legal reasons.
- **Reciprocity** — giving something of real value upfront (free tool, genuinely useful content) before asking for the sale increases conversion and is one of the few tactics with no ethical downside when done honestly.

## Where this breaks down

- Applying too many triggers simultaneously reads as manipulative and undermines trust — pick the 1-2 most relevant to the actual buying hesitation, not all of them at once.
- Any trigger built on a falsehood (fake countdown timers, fake reviews, fake scarcity) is a short-term lift and long-term trust and legal liability.

## Practical application checklist

Before shipping persuasive copy: is every claim true and verifiable? Does the trigger match the actual buying hesitation (a scarcity trigger doesn't fix a trust problem; a social-proof trigger doesn't fix a price objection)?
