# AI-SEO (GEO) — Ranking Inside AI Answer Engines

Distinct discipline from classic SEO: the goal is being retrieved, extracted, and cited by an LLM-based answer engine, not clicked from a results page.

## What AI answer engines actually pull from

- Content that answers a specific question directly and concisely near the top of the page, not buried after 800 words of preamble.
- Content structured in clearly extractable units — definition-style paragraphs, numbered steps, comparison tables — that can be lifted as a self-contained answer.
- Pages with clear, verifiable authority signals: named sources, specific data/numbers, publication/update dates.
- Content that exists in multiple corroborating places (the model's retrieval is more likely to trust and cite something that shows up consistently across sources).

## Structural tactics

1. **Answer the core question in the first 2-3 sentences.** Don't make the model excavate for the answer.
2. **Use explicit Q&A framing where natural** — a clear question as a heading followed by a direct answer is highly extractable.
3. **Include specific, checkable facts** — numbers, dates, named entities — generic/vague content is both less useful to humans and less likely to be selected as a citation.
4. **Keep a single canonical, up-to-date version** of any fact that might change (pricing, specs) — conflicting versions across your own pages hurts trust signals.

## What doesn't work here (unlike classic SEO)

- Keyword density tricks — irrelevant for how LLMs select and synthesize content.
- Backlink-count-driven authority alone — LLM retrieval weighs content quality and structure more heavily than raw link graph.

## Measurement

Classic rank tracking doesn't capture this. Track citation/mention rate by manually or programmatically querying target AI engines with representative user questions and checking whether/how the brand is cited.
