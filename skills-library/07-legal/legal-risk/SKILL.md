# Legal Risk Flagging — Exposure Identification

**Not legal advice — a first-pass flagging tool to identify what needs actual counsel review, before a decision is finalized.**

## Categories to scan for, depending on context

- **Contractual exposure** — commitments being made that create binding obligations without a clear internal owner tracking them, or that conflict with existing agreements.
- **Regulatory exposure** — activity that touches a regulated domain (financial services, data privacy, consumer protection, employment law) where a specific rule might apply — flag the domain even without knowing the precise rule, so counsel can confirm.
- **IP exposure** — use of third-party content, code, or branding without clear rights; product features that might infringe existing patents in a competitive space.
- **Data privacy exposure** — collection/storage/use of personal data, especially across borders, where different jurisdictions' rules (varying data protection regimes) might apply differently.
- **Employment/labor exposure** — classification of workers (employee vs. contractor), especially relevant for gig/marketplace-style products where misclassification risk is a live regulatory area in many jurisdictions.

## Output discipline

For each flagged item: what the specific risk is, why it's plausible given the facts described, and what it would take to resolve (usually: "confirm with counsel," sometimes a specific document/step). Never state a definitive legal conclusion ("this is/isn't compliant") — flag the question and the reasoning, and route the actual determination to a licensed professional.

## Especially important for cross-border/multi-jurisdiction plans

Explicitly call out which jurisdictions' rules might apply and where they could differ, rather than analyzing under a single assumed jurisdiction's framework by default — this is a common blind spot in fast-moving multi-market plans.
