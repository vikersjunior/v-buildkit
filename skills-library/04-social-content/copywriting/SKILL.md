# Copywriting — Page Copy Rewrites

For persuasive/explanatory web copy specifically — distinct from social posts (platform-format-driven) or ad copy (variation-testing-driven).

## Structural framework (problem-agitate-solve, adapted)

1. **Lead with the reader's situation, not the product.** Open with what the visitor is trying to do or the problem they have — not "We are a company that..."
2. **Make the value proposition scannable in the first screen.** A visitor deciding whether to keep reading needs to understand what this is and why it matters within a few seconds — headline + one supporting line should do this alone.
3. **Specific beats generic, every time.** "Cuts checkout time to under 90 seconds" beats "Fast, easy checkout." Vague adjectives (seamless, robust, innovative, cutting-edge) carry no information and should be replaced with a concrete claim or cut entirely.
4. **One CTA per section**, matched to that section's persuasion job (top of page: broad interest CTA; after proof section: stronger commitment CTA).

## Editing pass checklist

- Cut every sentence that could apply to any competitor's product unchanged — that sentence isn't doing work.
- Replace passive constructions with active, specific ones.
- Read it aloud — copy that's awkward spoken is usually awkward read too.
- Check reading level against the actual audience — a technical B2B audience tolerates denser copy than a consumer landing page.

## What "good" looks like

The reader should finish the page knowing exactly what the product does, who it's for, and what to do next — without having to infer any of the three.
