# Content Strategy — Topic Planning & Editorial Calendars

Planning what to create and why, before any individual piece gets written.

## Step 1: Anchor to a business goal, not "more content"

Every content plan should map to a specific outcome — organic search traffic, top-of-funnel awareness, sales enablement, retention/education for existing users. Content produced without a target outcome is the most common source of wasted effort.

## Step 2: Build topic clusters, not a random list

Pick 3-5 core topic pillars tied to what the business is actually trying to be known for. Under each pillar, map a set of supporting subtopics that a real target user would search for or care about. This creates topical authority (useful for SEO) and avoids scattershot, disconnected content.

## Step 3: Sequence by leverage, not chronology

Prioritize pieces that: (a) target genuine search demand with low existing competition, (b) directly support an active sales/product motion, or (c) can be repurposed across multiple formats (one deep piece → social posts, an email, a video script) for compounding output per unit of effort.

## Step 4: Build the calendar

For each planned piece: target topic, target audience/intent, target format, target publish date, and the specific goal it serves. A calendar without the "why" column degrades into a content mill.

## Step 5: Plan for repurposing, not one-and-done

Every substantial piece should have a repurposing plan attached at creation time — which social platforms, which email sequence, which internal doc — rather than deciding after the fact whether it's "worth" repurposing.

## Anti-pattern

Calendars built around a arbitrary publishing cadence ("we post 3x a week") with topics backfilled to hit the schedule — this produces filler, not strategy.
