# Social — Platform-Specific Post Formatting

One piece of content reformatted identically across platforms underperforms everywhere. This skill enforces platform-native formatting.

## Platform conventions

- **X/Twitter** — front-load the point in the first line (feeds truncate); short, punchy sentence structure; threads for anything needing more than ~280 characters of development, with each tweet in the thread able to stand alone if seen out of context.
- **LinkedIn** — a strong hook line before the "see more" fold (roughly the first 2-3 lines); longer-form narrative/insight structure performs better than punchy one-liners here; professional register but not corporate-stiff — personal narrative with a point outperforms pure announcement copy.
- **Instagram** — caption supports the visual, doesn't duplicate it; hook in the first line before the "more" truncation; hashtags as discovery tool (relevant, not maxed-out spam) placed at the end or in first comment.
- **TikTok** — caption is secondary to the video hook itself (first 1-2 seconds of video carry the weight); short, conversational caption; trend/sound relevance matters more than caption polish.

## Universal structural checklist regardless of platform

1. Hook in the first line — assume most of the audience never sees past it.
2. One idea per post — a post trying to make three points lands none of them.
3. Explicit, platform-native CTA (comment/save/share/link-in-bio) — don't assume the audience knows what action to take.

## Ghana/African-market note

WhatsApp is often the dominant distribution channel, not a traditional "social platform" — format for forwarding (self-contained message, works without external context) rather than assuming a feed-based discovery model.
