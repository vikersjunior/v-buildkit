# Pitch Deck — Investor Deck Construction

Content and narrative structure for a fundraising deck — pair with the pptx skill for the actual slide-file build.

## Standard slide sequence (adapt order to what's strongest, don't force weak slides early)

1. **Problem** — specific, real, and ideally something the audience has felt themselves or can immediately recognize as real. Vague or manufactured problems are the most common early red flag investors flag.
2. **Solution** — how you solve it, kept concrete — what the product actually does, not abstract mission language.
3. **Market size** — bottom-up sizing (real addressable customer count × realistic price point) is more credible than a top-down "if we capture 1% of a trillion-dollar market" claim, which investors have learned to discount heavily.
4. **Product** — screenshots/demo of the actual thing, not a mockup dressed as finished — investors specifically probe for this gap.
5. **Traction** — the strongest evidence available: revenue, users, growth rate, retention, signed LOIs — whatever's genuinely strong, led with, not buried.
6. **Business model** — how this makes money, unit economics if available (CAC, LTV, margin) — investors will ask if this slide doesn't answer it first.
7. **Go-to-market** — the specific, credible plan for acquiring the next cohort of customers, not a generic "marketing and sales" statement.
8. **Competition** — an honest positioning map, not a claim of having no competitors (a "no competitors" claim reads as either naive market analysis or a market too small to matter, both bad signals).
9. **Team** — why this specific team is credible to execute this specific plan — relevant background, not just titles.
10. **Financials/ask** — what's being raised, what it's for (specific use of funds, not vague "scaling"), and the runway it buys.

## Narrative discipline

The deck should read as one coherent argument, not ten disconnected slides — each slide should logically require the next. If a slide could be removed without breaking the argument, cut it.

## Regional fundraising note

For Ghana/African-market fundraising specifically, be explicit and realistic about infrastructure dependencies (payment rail access, connectivity assumptions, regulatory environment) rather than assuming Western infrastructure defaults — investors sophisticated in the region will probe this specifically, and glossing over it reads as inexperience with the actual market.
