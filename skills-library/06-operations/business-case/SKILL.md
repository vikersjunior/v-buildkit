# Business Case — ROI & Investment Justification

Structured argument for why a specific investment should be made, with the numbers to back it.

## Structure

1. **Problem/opportunity statement** — what's the current state, and what's it costing (in money, time, risk, or lost opportunity) to not act.
2. **Proposed solution** — what's being proposed, at a level of specificity that makes the cost estimate credible (not "improve efficiency" — a specific initiative with a defined scope).
3. **Cost estimate** — all-in cost: direct spend, implementation time/labor cost, ongoing maintenance cost — a business case that only counts the sticker price and misses implementation/ongoing cost systematically understates true cost.
4. **Benefit estimate** — quantified wherever honestly possible (cost savings, revenue impact, time saved × loaded cost of that time), with the underlying assumption stated explicitly for each number, not just a bare final figure — a reader should be able to see and challenge the assumption chain.
5. **ROI/payback calculation** — (benefit − cost) / cost, and/or payback period (time until cumulative benefit exceeds cumulative cost). Show the calculation, not just the conclusion.
6. **Risks and mitigations** — what could make this not deliver the projected benefit, and what's being done about each risk. A business case with no stated risks reads as either naive or not rigorously examined — neither builds credibility.
7. **Alternatives considered** — including explicitly the "do nothing" alternative and its cost, since inaction has a cost too and should be part of the comparison, not an implicit default.

## Credibility discipline

Use conservative, defensible assumptions rather than best-case numbers — a business case that survives scrutiny under conservative assumptions is far more persuasive than one that only works in the optimistic case. State assumptions as ranges where genuine uncertainty exists, rather than false precision.
